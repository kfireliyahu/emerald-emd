    Emerald

    Where what you and an AI worked out goes to stay true — in a file you own.

    I kept my notes in Notion for years. It was clunky, and everything I wrote sat on their servers. Obsidian gave me my files back, but no structure: a note is a note, and nothing says when it stopped being true. Excel had the structure, and it held up right until things started to connect, a receipt to a charge, a paycheck to the hours behind it. Then it stopped adding up, quietly.

    Emerald is what I built instead.

    Integrating known concepts into new ideas

    Excel, other note taking apps, all claim to give order to chaos. AI and LLMs are part of the new chaos. They aim to give the user tools working on new ideas, increasing output, but not quality.

    Emerald gives you a better way to interact and use AI to its full potential. But even if you don't use the AI, you still have a powerful tool for organizing your data.

    Infinite possibilities

    Emerald is a framework for your own formats, and for the AI to read them. You can make a format for a grocery charge, a new project, a book, a paycheck, milestones, plans, ideas. You can make one for anything you want to keep true.

    You can make packs of your own formats, integrating to ones provided by Emerald. The development pack allows multi-agent communication, long term memory, continuous work, and a way for one session to hand what it found to the next.

    The ledger

    The ledger is the pack Emerald grew out of: a complete personal-finance application, kept as declared formats. It reads the documents you already get, a card statement, a bank export, a payslip, a fund report, and files every charge. It knows a transfer from a spend and a monthly bill from a one-off, and every total it shows is a sum you can open to the rows.

    Nothing connects to your bank. You give it the files, and it keeps them adding up.

    The development pack

    The development pack is a project's memory, as formats: milestones, units of work, decisions, open questions, and an inbox another project's session can write to. A model working on your project reads what was already decided before it builds, writes down what you decide next, and the next session, or another agent, starts from those rows rather than from a summary of a chat.

    Emerald is built this way. Its own notes are a document in this pack, and the third tour above plays on one like it.

    Locality and security

    Emerald runs on your machine, with no account and no server, and sends nothing anywhere itself. You can read it with ordinary tools, and you can back it up to a cloud service if you want. It is yours, and it stays that way.
